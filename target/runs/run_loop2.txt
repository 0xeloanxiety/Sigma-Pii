[2] HIGH  —  Zero step duration permanently disables claims
    location: SecondSwap_VestingDeployer.sol:115-116 and SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:133, 172-174
    what:     The deployer only checks that startTime < endTime and steps > 0, while SecondSwap_StepVesting computes stepDuration as (endTime - startTime) / numOfSteps without requiring the duration to be at least the number of steps. If the duration is smaller than the number of steps, stepDuration becomes zero. Once the vesting starts, claimable() divides by stepDuration, causing every claimable() and claim() call to revert.
    impact:   All allocations in a vesting plan with this configuration become permanently unclaimable, locking the escrowed vesting tokens.
    exploit:
      1. A token owner deploys a vesting plan with startTime < endTime but endTime - startTime < steps, which the deployer accepts.
      2. The token owner creates a vesting allocation and funds it through createVesting.
      3. After startTime, the beneficiary calls claim().
      4. claimable() executes elapsedTime / stepDuration with stepDuration == 0 and reverts, so the beneficiary cannot claim any tokens.

[3] HIGH  —  Vesting transfers can make beneficiaries claim more than their remaining allocation
    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:223-232
    what:     After reducing a grantor's totalAmount during transferVesting, the contract recalculates releaseRate as grantorVesting.totalAmount / numOfSteps. This uses the post-transfer total allocation and the full number of steps, while amountClaimed and stepsClaimed remain unchanged. The rate must instead be based on the grantor's remaining unclaimed amount and remaining steps. Because claimable() uses releaseRate directly in its non-final branch without capping it against totalAmount - amountClaimed, a transfer after prior claims can cause amountClaimed to exceed totalAmount.
    impact:   A beneficiary can withdraw more tokens than their unclaimed allocation, draining tokens belonging to other vesting allocations from the shared escrow. Subsequent claims can also permanently revert because the final-claim subtraction underflows.
    exploit:
      1. A user obtains a 100-token allocation in a 10-step plan and claims the first step, receiving 10 tokens. Their state is approximately totalAmount = 100, amountClaimed = 10, and stepsClaimed = 1.
      2. The user buys or otherwise holds the allocation through the marketplace so that their recorded bought amount is at least 90, then lists 90 tokens. The marketplace's sell-limit calculation permits selling up to the bought amount, and the vesting manager transfers 90 tokens out.
      3. transferVesting reduces totalAmount to 10 but leaves amountClaimed at 10 and sets releaseRate to 10 / 10 = 1.
      4. At each of steps 2 through 9, the beneficiary calls claim(). The contract pays 1 token per step even though their remaining unclaimed allocation is already zero, resulting in amountClaimed = 18 while totalAmount = 10.
      5. At the final step, claimable() attempts totalAmount - amountClaimed and reverts due to underflow.

[6] MEDIUM  —  Claiming at the schedule end can set stepsClaimed above numOfSteps
    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:167-185, 193-201, 288-303
    what:     claimable() calculates currentStep as elapsedTime / stepDuration without capping it to numOfSteps. For schedules whose duration is not evenly divisible by the number of steps, currentStep at endTime can exceed numOfSteps. The final-branch claimable amount is capped correctly, but claim() still adds the excessive claimableSteps to vesting.stepsClaimed. For example, an 8-second schedule with 3 steps has stepDuration 2 and currentStep 4 at endTime, so claiming sets stepsClaimed to 4 even though numOfSteps is 3.     impact:   The beneficiary's vesting state becomes inconsistent and future vesting additions to that address revert permanently.     exploit:
      1. Deploy a valid vesting plan with startTime < endTime, for example an 8-second duration and 3 steps; deployment accepts this because only steps > 0 is checked.
      2. Create an allocation for a beneficiary.
      3. Wait until endTime and call claim(). stepDuration is 2, currentStep is 4, and claim() increments stepsClaimed by 4, leaving stepsClaimed = 4 while numOfSteps = 3.
      4. Have the token issuer call createVesting() for the same beneficiary, or have the beneficiary purchase an allocation through the marketplace.
      5. The existing-beneficiary path evaluates numOfSteps - stepsClaimed and underflows, causing the allocation creation or marketplace purchase to revert.

    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:288-307 and 193-202
    what:     _createVesting records _totalAmount in totalAmount after calling token.safeTransferFrom(tokenIssuer, address(this), _totalAmount), without checking the contract's actual balance increase.       3. Advance time to the end of the vesting schedule and call claim(). claimable() returns the full remaining recorded allocation of 100.
