[1] HIGH  —  Partial vesting transfers enable overclaiming and drain other allocations
    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:216-232
    what:     transferVesting reduces totalAmount but leaves amountClaimed unchanged and resets releaseRate to totalAmount / numOfSteps instead of recalculating it from the remaining amount and remaining steps. claimable() then pays releaseRate * claimableSteps without capping the result against totalAmount - amountClaimed except at the final step.
    impact:   A beneficiary who has already claimed part of a vesting schedule can list or otherwise transfer part of the remaining allocation and then claim more than the adjusted allocation. The excess is paid from the vesting contract's pooled token balance, potentially underfunding buyers or other beneficiaries.
    exploit:
      1. Create a 10-step vesting schedule with totalAmount 100 for a seller and allow the seller to claim the first 5 steps, resulting in amountClaimed 50 and stepsClaimed 5.
      2. List 20 tokens through the marketplace. With the default 20% max sell percentage, this amount is permitted. transferVesting reduces totalAmount to 80 but sets releaseRate to 80 / 10 = 8.
      3. Complete a purchase so the marketplace-held 20 tokens are transferred to the buyer's vesting allocation rather than returned to the seller.
      4. At steps 6 through 9, have the seller claim 8 tokens per step. The seller receives 32 tokens even though only 30 tokens remained in the seller's adjusted allocation.
      5. The seller's amountClaimed becomes 82 while totalAmount is only 80. The excess 2 tokens are taken from the vesting contract balance and the buyer's 20-token allocation becomes underfunded.

[2] HIGH  —  Vesting schedules with more steps than seconds are permanently unclaimable
    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:117-134 and 161-174
    what:     The constructor computes stepDuration as (_endTime - _startTime) / _numOfSteps without requiring the duration to be at least the number of steps. For a positive duration smaller than _numOfSteps, stepDuration becomes zero. claimable() then divides elapsedTime by stepDuration.
    impact:   Every non-empty vesting schedule created with stepDuration == 0 cannot be claimed because claimable() always reverts after the start time. The marketplace does not validate this invariant, so buyers can pay for vesting allocations that are permanently inaccessible.
    exploit:
      1. Deploy a vesting schedule through SecondSwap_VestingDeployer with startTime 1000, endTime 1001, and steps 2. The deployer only checks steps > 0, so deployment succeeds and stepDuration becomes zero.
      2. Create a vesting allocation for a beneficiary and make the plan sellable.
      3. List the allocation and have a buyer complete a marketplace purchase.
      4. After startTime, call claim() for the seller or buyer. claimable() executes elapsedTime / stepDuration with a zero divisor and reverts, permanently preventing claims.
