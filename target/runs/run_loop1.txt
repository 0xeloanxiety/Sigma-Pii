[2] HIGH  —  Internal transfers can make vesting claims exceed the remaining allocation
    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:216-232
    what:     When transferring an allocation, the grantor's totalAmount is reduced but amountClaimed is unchanged. A grantor who has already claimed tokens can therefore receive more future claimable tokens than remain in its reduced allocation.
    impact:   A seller can overclaim tokens after listing part of an already-partially-claimed allocation. The excess is paid from tokens backing the manager's or a buyer's allocation, causing insolvency and making other users' claims fail.
    exploit:

      2. List 20 tokens through the marketplace. The manager transfers 20 tokens from the beneficiary, reducing totalAmount to 80 while amountClaimed remains 50 and setting releaseRate to 8.
      3. Have a buyer purchase the listed 20 tokens so the manager's allocation is transferred to the buyer.
      4. At step 9, the seller calls claim. claimableSteps is 4 and the flawed releaseRate causes claimableAmount to be 32, although only 30 tokens remain in the seller's allocation.
      5. The seller receives 32 tokens, consuming 2 tokens backing the buyer's 20-token allocation. The buyer's later claim for the full allocation can no longer succeed because the vesting contract is short by 2 tokens.

[6] MEDIUM  —  Truncated step duration can record more claimed steps than the schedule contains
    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:133 and SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:172-181
    what:     The constructor stores `stepDuration = (endTime - startTime) / numOfSteps` using integer division. `claimable` then computes `currentStep = elapsedTime / stepDuration` without capping `currentStep` to `numOfSteps`; only `currentTime` is capped to `endTime`. For valid nonzero-duration schedules where the division truncates sufficiently, `currentStep` at `endTime` exceeds `numOfSteps`. For example, an 8-second schedule with 3 steps has `stepDuration == 2`, so `currentStep == 4` at the end. The final claim adds four steps to `stepsClaimed`, even though the schedule has only three.
    impact:   The vesting record enters an invalid state with `stepsClaimed > numOfSteps`. Subsequent attempts to add an allocation to that beneficiary through `createVesting` permanently revert at `numOfSteps - stepsClaimed`, preventing legitimate vesting top-ups for the beneficiary. The reported number of claimed steps also violates the schedule accounting invariant.
    exploit:
      1. Deploy a valid vesting plan with `startTime = 0`, `endTime = 8`, and `numOfSteps = 3`; the deployer accepts these parameters and computes `stepDuration = 2`.
      2. Create a vesting allocation for a beneficiary.
      3. Wait until `endTime` and call `claim()` for the beneficiary.
      4. `claimable` computes `currentStep = 8 / 2 = 4`, `claimableSteps = 4`, and returns the full remaining allocation.
      5. The claim stores `stepsClaimed = 4`, exceeding `numOfSteps = 3`.
      6. Call `createVesting` for the same beneficiary with any positive amount. The existing-beneficiary branch evaluates `numOfSteps - stepsClaimed` and reverts due to unsigned underflow.

[7] MEDIUM  —  Fee-on-transfer payment tokens can drain marketplace reserves
    location: SecondSwap_Marketplace.sol:476-492 (_handleTransfers)
    what:     The marketplace assumes that `safeTransferFrom(msg.sender, address(this), baseAmount + buyerFeeTotal)` credits the marketplace with exactly the requested amount. It never measures the marketplace's balance before and after the transfer. It then unconditionally transfers `baseAmount - sellerFeeTotal` to the seller and `buyerFeeTotal + sellerFeeTotal` to the fee collector. A fee-on-transfer or otherwise deflationary token can therefore credit less than the amount requested while the marketplace still pays the full amount.
    impact:   If a supported payment currency charges a transfer fee, each purchase can consume pre-existing marketplace reserves to cover the difference between the requested and received amounts. Once reserves are exhausted, purchases revert; the lost reserves accrue to the payment token's fee recipient or burn address. `addCoin` allows such tokens because its token validation is commented out.
    exploit:
      1. The administrator enables a fee-on-transfer ERC-20 through `addCoin`, which does not reject such tokens.
      2. A seller creates a listing using that supported currency.
      3. The marketplace accumulates a balance of the currency from prior activity or an administrator deposit.
      4. A buyer approves the marketplace and calls `spotPurchase`.
      5. The token's `transferFrom` credits the marketplace with less than `baseAmount + buyerFeeTotal`, but returns successfully.
      6. `_handleTransfers` nevertheless transfers the full seller proceeds and fee amount, causing the marketplace's existing balance to cover the shortfall. Repeat purchases drain the reserve.

[8] MEDIUM  —  Vesting manager pooling corrupts transferred vesting schedules
    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:288-303, SecondSwap_VestingManager.sol:149-163
    what:     All listed allocations for a vesting plan are pooled under the manager address. When the manager already has an allocation, `_createVesting` increases its `totalAmount` but retains the manager's existing `stepsClaimed` and ignores the incoming grantor's `_stepsClaimed`. Purchases then transfer from this pooled manager allocation and initialize the buyer using the pooled `stepsClaimed`. Consequently, the vesting progress of the first seller whose allocation entered the pool is applied to every later seller's allocation.
    impact:   Buyers can receive allocations with an incorrect vesting schedule. Depending on listing order, a buyer can claim a later-stage seller's allocation substantially earlier than its source schedule permits, or a fully unclaimed seller's allocation can be delayed until later steps. This breaks vesting-time guarantees and can make purchased tokens temporarily unavailable or prematurely claimable.
    exploit:
      1. Deploy a ten-step vesting plan and wait until step 8.
      2. Have seller A, who has not claimed any steps, list a permitted amount first. `transferVesting` initializes the manager's plan allocation with `stepsClaimed == 0`.
      3. Have seller B, who has claimed the first eight steps and has an unclaimed balance, list an amount. The manager already has an allocation, so `_createVesting` aggregates B's amount while retaining `stepsClaimed == 0` instead of B's value of 8.
      4. Buy B's listing. `completePurchase` transfers from the manager and initializes the buyer with the manager's `stepsClaimed == 0`.
      5. At step 8, the buyer can claim approximately 80% of B's transferred amount immediately, even though B's remaining allocation was at step 8 and should inherit that progress. Reversing the listing order causes the opposite failure: A's newly listed allocation inherits `stepsClaimed == 8` and is delayed.
