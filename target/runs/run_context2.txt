[1] HIGH  —  Vesting schedules with zero step duration permanently disable claims
    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:133 and SecondSwap_VestingDeployer.sol:104-130
    what:     The deployer validates only startTime < endTime and steps > 0, but does not require endTime - startTime >= steps. The constructor computes stepDuration = (endTime - startTime) / numOfSteps, which becomes zero when the number of steps exceeds the schedule duration. Once the schedule starts, claimable() executes elapsedTime / stepDuration at line 173 and reverts due to division by zero.
    impact:   All allocations in such a schedule become unclaimable through claim(), permanently locking the vested tokens unless an authorized issuer or deployer manually transfers the allocations elsewhere. A token owner can also sell allocations from the malformed schedule, causing buyers to receive vesting rights that cannot be claimed.
    exploit:
      1. Have a mapped token owner call deployVesting with startTime = T, endTime = T + 1, and steps = 2.
      2. Create a vesting allocation in the resulting contract and list or sell that allocation through the marketplace.
      3. After T + 1, call claim() as the beneficiary.
      4. claimable() divides by stepDuration == 0 and reverts, so the beneficiary cannot claim any allocation.

[2] MEDIUM  —  Non-divisible vesting durations release allocations early and overshoot steps
    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:167-181
    what:     stepDuration is rounded down in the constructor. claimable() computes currentStep as (currentTime - startTime) / stepDuration but never caps currentStep to numOfSteps. For example, a ten-second schedule with four steps gets a two-second stepDuration, so currentStep reaches four at t = start + 8, two seconds before endTime. At endTime, currentStep can exceed numOfSteps and the returned claimableSteps can exceed the configured step count.
    impact:   Beneficiaries can claim their entire allocation before endTime. When an oversized claimableSteps value is stored in stepsClaimed, later additions or transfers to that beneficiary can revert because _createVesting subtracts stepsClaimed from numOfSteps, permanently blocking subsequent allocations for that address.
    exploit:
      1. Deploy a schedule with startTime = T, endTime = T + 10, and steps = 4.
      2. Create a beneficiary allocation.
      3. At T + 8, call claim(); currentStep is already 4, so claimable() returns the entire remaining allocation even though endTime has not arrived.
      4. Alternatively, wait until T + 10 and claim; currentStep is 5, so stepsClaimed is increased beyond numOfSteps.
      5. Attempt to create or transfer another allocation to the same beneficiary; _createVesting evaluates numOfSteps - stepsClaimed and reverts.

[3] MEDIUM  —  Transfers through an already-funded manager schedule corrupt vesting release timing
    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:216-232 and 288-303
    what:     transferVesting passes the source's stepsClaimed value to _createVesting, but when the beneficiary already has a vesting record, _createVesting ignores the incoming _stepsClaimed and recalculates using the beneficiary's existing stepsClaimed. The vesting manager is itself the beneficiary for all listed allocations, so allocations from sellers at different claim stages are merged into a single schedule and later purchases inherit the manager's unrelated step count.
    impact:   A purchased allocation can become claimable earlier or later than the source allocation's vesting schedule. In particular, if the manager first receives an unclaimed allocation with stepsClaimed = 0 and later receives an allocation from a seller who has already claimed eight of ten steps, a buyer of the latter allocation receives stepsClaimed = 0 and can claim most of it immediately instead of waiting for the final two steps.
    exploit:
      1. Use a ten-step vesting plan and have seller B list an unclaimed allocation first, causing the manager's vesting record to have stepsClaimed = 0.
      2. Have seller A claim the first eight steps of another allocation, leaving only the final two-step allocation available, and list that remaining allocation.
      3. The manager transfers seller A's allocation using _createVesting(..., _stepsClaimed = 8, true), but because the manager already has a record, its stepsClaimed remains 0.
      4. Purchase seller A's listing at step eight and call claim() as the buyer; the buyer's new record inherits stepsClaimed = 0 and can claim accrued early-step amounts that should remain locked until the final steps.
