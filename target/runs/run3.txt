[1] HIGH  —  Post-claim vesting transfers allow beneficiaries to claim beyond their allocation
    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:216-230
    what:     transferVesting reduces totalAmount but leaves amountClaimed and stepsClaimed unchanged, then recalculates releaseRate as grantorVesting.totalAmount / numOfSteps. After a beneficiary has already claimed tokens, this rate is not based on the remaining amount or remaining steps. claimable() subsequently calculates intermediate claims as releaseRate * claimableSteps without capping against totalAmount - amountClaimed. For example, with a 100-token, 10-step schedule, after claiming 50 tokens over five steps, listing 20 remaining tokens reduces totalAmount to 80 while amountClaimed remains 50 and sets releaseRate to 8. The beneficiary can then claim 8 tokens per step even though only 30 tokens remain, causing amountClaimed to exceed totalAmount.
    impact:   A seller can claim tokens that have been transferred into the marketplace allocation. This consumes tokens reserved for marketplace buyers or other beneficiaries, causing later claims to revert or leaving the vesting contract underfunded.
    exploit:
      1. Create a 100-token vesting schedule with 10 steps and create the corresponding vesting allocation for the attacker.
      2. Wait until five steps have elapsed and claim the first 50 tokens.
      3. List 20 tokens through the marketplace. The default 20% max-sell setting permits this, and the marketplace manager calls transferVesting, reducing totalAmount from 100 to 80 while amountClaimed remains 50.
      4. At the next vesting steps, call claim repeatedly. releaseRate is now 80 / 10 = 8, so claimable() returns 8 tokens per intermediate step despite only 30 tokens remaining.
      5. After four such claims, amountClaimed becomes 82 while totalAmount is only 80, stealing two tokens from the marketplace allocation and causing subsequent accounting or claims to fail.

[2] HIGH  —  Zero step duration permanently locks vesting allocations
    location: SecondSwap_StepVesting.sol:117-134 and SecondSwap_VestingDeployer.sol:104-117
    what:     The deployer validates only startTime < endTime and steps > 0. It does not require endTime - startTime >= steps. The StepVesting constructor computes stepDuration = (endTime - startTime) / numOfSteps, which becomes zero when the number of steps exceeds the schedule duration. Once the schedule starts, claimable() divides elapsedTime by stepDuration at line 173, causing every claimable() and claim() call to revert permanently because stepDuration is immutable.
    impact:   Any allocations created in a vesting schedule with more steps than seconds in its duration cannot be claimed, permanently locking the deposited token balance in the vesting contract.
    exploit:
      1. The token owner calls deployVesting with, for example, startTime = 1000, endTime = 1002, and steps = 3. All deployer checks pass.
      2. Create one or more vesting allocations in the deployed StepVesting contract, transferring tokens into it.
      3. After startTime, a beneficiary calls claim().
      4. claimable() reaches elapsedTime / stepDuration, where stepDuration is zero, and the call reverts.
      5. Because the duration fields are immutable and there is no recovery or alternate claim path, the allocated tokens remain locked.
